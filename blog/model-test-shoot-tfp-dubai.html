Model Test Shoots (TFP) in Dubai:
What to Expect

Written by Hatim
Published August 2026
Read time 7 min

If you've been told to "get some TFP" or find a photographer for a "test shoot" and weren't quite sure what that meant, you're not missing something obvious. It's a term that gets used constantly around new models and explained properly almost never.

Here's what it actually means, when it's worth doing, and what a fair one looks like in Dubai specifically.

What TFP actually means

TFP stands for Trade For Photos, sometimes written as Trade For Print from its film-era origins. It describes a shoot where no money changes hands: the model trades their time in front of the camera, and the photographer trades their time shooting and editing, and both walk away with images they can use.

"Test shoot" is the broader term for the same idea. It's a collaborative session built for portfolio material and experience rather than a paid commercial booking, and in everyday use the two terms are interchangeable.

The trade, spelled out

The model gets usable images for digitals, a portfolio, or a comp card, plus real experience being directed. The photographer gets images for their own portfolio too. Neither side is doing the other a favour; it's a genuine two-way exchange, which is why the images should actually be good on both ends.

When a test shoot makes sense

A test shoot is a good fit in a few specific situations:

  • You're new to modeling and need your first real set of images to apply to agencies with
  • You have a specific gap in your portfolio, a look or category you haven't shot yet
  • You want to build experience in front of a camera before a paid booking
  • You and a photographer both want to try a concept neither of you has shot before

It's a weaker fit if you already have strong, current material and just need more volume, or if you're being asked to trade your time for images that clearly only benefit the photographer. A test shoot should leave both people with something they didn't have before.

What to expect from the session

A well-run test shoot doesn't look much different from a paid one in structure. What changes is the payment, not the professionalism. You should still expect a clear plan for the shoot, direction throughout, and edited images delivered afterward rather than a folder of untouched files.

What often differs from a paid booking is scope: a test shoot may cover fewer looks, a shorter session, or a single location rather than a full production. That's a reasonable trade-off. What isn't reasonable is vagueness about what you'll actually receive.

Questions to ask before you agree

  1. What will I actually receive, and how many images? Get a number, not "a few nice ones."
  2. What's the turnaround time? A test shoot that never delivers final images isn't a trade, it's free content.
  3. Who owns usage rights? Clarify whether the photographer can post the images publicly, and whether that's fine with you.
  4. Is hair and makeup included, or is that on you? This affects how much you need to prepare and bring.
  5. Where and how long? A studio session and an outdoor half-day shoot are different commitments; know which one you're agreeing to.

None of this is unusual to ask. A photographer who takes test shoots seriously will have straightforward answers to all five before you even ask.

Finding a test shoot in Dubai

Agency networks, model community groups, and photographers who openly say they take on test shoots are the usual routes in. Before agreeing to anything, look at the photographer's existing portfolio the same way you'd want them to look at yours: does the work look like something you'd want your name attached to?

A test shoot is still a real shoot. It's worth being just as selective about who you work with as you would be for a paid booking, since the images you walk away with are what agencies and clients will actually see.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does TFP mean in modeling?
TFP stands for Trade For Photos (sometimes Trade For Print). It describes a collaborative shoot where the model and photographer both contribute time and skill in exchange for usable images, rather than a paid booking.
Is a test shoot free for models?
Usually, yes. In a true TFP arrangement, no money changes hands. Both sides are trading their time and work: the model's time in front of the camera, and the photographer's time shooting and editing, for images each side can use.
What should a model get out of a test shoot?
A set of usable, edited images suitable for digitals, a portfolio update, or a comp card, and, for newer models, real experience being directed in front of a camera. What you actually receive should be agreed before the shoot, not assumed.
How do I find a TFP photographer in Dubai?
Through modeling agency networks, model community groups, and photographers who publicly state they take on test shoots. Always check a photographer's existing portfolio before agreeing, since a TFP shoot still needs to be worth your time.
What questions should I ask before a TFP shoot?
Confirm what images you will receive and how many, the expected turnaround time, who owns usage rights to the final images, whether hair and makeup are included or your responsibility, and the shoot location and length.
Is TFP the same as a test shoot?
In practice the terms are used interchangeably. Test shoot is the more general term for a collaborative, experience or portfolio-building session; TFP specifically describes the trade-based payment structure behind it.
