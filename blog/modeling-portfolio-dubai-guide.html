Modeling Guide

How to Build a Modeling Portfolio
in Dubai: Complete 2026 Guide

Written by Hatim
Published July 2026
Read time 10 min

Dubai is one of the most active modelling markets in the Middle East. Agencies here work across fashion, commercial, fitness, editorial, and luxury: and almost all of them require a portfolio before they'll even take your call.

I've photographed models at every stage: first-timers who've never been in front of a camera, experienced models refreshing their look, and working professionals updating their books for new agency submissions. The questions are usually the same. How many photos do I need? What should I wear? Which photographer should I pick? What do agencies actually want to see?

This guide answers all of it. Honestly, from the photographer's side of the lens.

Start with your niche

Before you book a single shoot, decide what kind of modelling you're pursuing. This determines everything: the style of photos you need, the outfits you bring, and how the images should be lit and edited.

  • Fashion / Editorial: Strong angles, directional light, avant-garde styling. Agencies want to see how you wear clothes and move on camera.
  • Commercial / Lifestyle: Natural expressions, relatable energy. Think brand campaigns, product shoots, everyday scenarios.
  • Fitness: Physique, movement, outdoor or gym settings. Athletic wear in natural light.
  • Luxury / High-end: Refined, polished. Minimal styling, clean backgrounds. The portfolio needs to feel expensive.
  • Runway: Full-length shots that showcase proportions, posture, and walk. Less about expression, more about line.

Most people fall somewhere between two of these. That's fine: but your portfolio should lean toward one direction rather than trying to cover everything at once.

How many photos do you need

A starting portfolio in Dubai needs between 10 and 15 strong images. That's it. Agencies don't want to scroll through 60 shots: they're looking at dozens of submissions a week and they make decisions in seconds.

The agency rule

10 exceptional photos will always outperform 40 average ones. Every image in your portfolio should earn its place. If you're hesitating about whether to include a shot, leave it out.

Within those 10 to 15 images, you need variety. A typical submission package should include:

  • 2–3 clean headshots (different expressions, at least one natural smile)
  • 2–3 full-length shots showing your proportions clearly
  • 3–5 fashion or editorial images showing range and personality
  • 1–2 natural / lifestyle shots
  • 1 comp card layout (more on this below)

What to wear

Bring at least three outfits to your portfolio shoot. More is better, but three is the minimum that gives you real variety without the session becoming chaotic.

  • Look 1: Clean and fitted: Something simple and fitted in a neutral tone (white, black, cream, grey). This is your "agency clean look": agencies want to see your body, not your outfit. Think fitted jeans and a plain top, or a simple dress.
  • Look 2: Fashion forward: Something that shows your style and personality. This could be structured, editorial, street, or elegant: whatever feels authentic to the direction you're pursuing.
  • Look 3: Casual / lifestyle: Something relaxed. Natural, approachable. This is for the commercial shots that show you as a real person rather than a mannequin.

Avoid heavy logos, busy patterns, and anything that pulls attention away from your face and posture. Colour is fine, but stay away from anything neon or that clashes with your skin tone on camera.

The most common mistake I see: people come to a portfolio shoot wearing what they'd wear to a party. That's often the wrong call. Unless party wear is your niche, keep the focus on clean, versatile looks.

Hair, makeup and grooming

For a portfolio shoot, professional hair and makeup is worth the investment. This isn't about looking different from how you normally look: it's about looking like the best, most polished version of yourself. Good makeup photographs completely differently from everyday makeup, and a professional knows how to work with camera lighting.

Ideally, plan at least two different makeup looks across your session: one clean and natural, one with more definition. This gives your portfolio visual range without needing to change outfits every ten minutes.

For men: a clean shave or a well-groomed beard. Moisturised skin. Clean, styled hair. It sounds basic, but the difference between a groomed look and an ungroomed one is immediately visible on camera.

Choosing the right photographer

This is the decision that most affects the quality of your portfolio. The wrong photographer can produce technically acceptable images that still won't get you signed anywhere. The right one understands how agencies think, how to light a model for submission, and how to direct someone who may not have much experience in front of a camera.

When evaluating photographers in Dubai, look at:

  1. Their existing portfolio Do their portrait images have the look and feel your target agencies work with? If a photographer specialises in product photography, they're likely not the right choice for your modelling book.
  2. Their experience directing models Ask directly. Can they guide you through poses? Will they tell you when something isn't working? A good photographer is also a good director: they should make you feel comfortable and push you to try things.
  3. Editing style Look at the finished images in their portfolio, not just the behind-the-scenes shots. Are the edits clean and professional? Over-filtered or over-retouched images won't serve you well with agencies.
  4. What's included Confirm the number of final edited images, turnaround time, usage rights, and whether RAW files are available. In Dubai, most photographers deliver 20 to 30 selects from a 2-hour session, with 10 to 15 fully retouched finals.
  5. Budget vs value Portfolio sessions in Dubai range from AED 800 to AED 3,500+. The cheapest option is rarely the best value for a professional tool. Prioritise quality over cost.

Locations in Dubai

Dubai gives you a remarkable variety of settings within a short drive of each other: and location matters for a portfolio.

  • Studio: Clean, controlled, professional. Essential for agency headshots and full-length looks. Every portfolio needs at least some studio images.
  • Downtown / DIFC: Urban architecture, glass towers, strong geometric backgrounds. Great for fashion-forward editorial looks.
  • Al Fahidi / Old Dubai: Textured walls, traditional architecture, warm tones. Beautiful for a more artistic or culturally grounded feel.
  • The Desert: Golden light, vast space, dramatic. Shoot within the first hour after sunrise or the last hour before sunset: midday desert light is unusable.
  • Beach / JBR / La Mer: Natural light, movement, lifestyle feel. Strong for commercial and fitness portfolios.
Dubai-specific note

Check permit requirements before shooting in public spaces. Many popular locations in Dubai require a filming/photography permit, and enforcement is unpredictable. A photographer who knows the city will already have this handled or will advise you in advance.

What agencies in Dubai actually look for

I've worked with models who've signed to agencies after their portfolio shoot and spoken to bookers directly over the years. Here's what consistently comes up:

  • Natural expression: Forced smiles and stiff poses read immediately. Agencies want to see someone who looks comfortable, not someone who looks like they're trying hard to look comfortable.
  • Range: At least two or three clearly different moods within the portfolio. Not just different outfits, but different energy and expression.
  • Clean skin representation: Not heavily retouched to the point where your actual skin texture is gone, but polished. Agencies need to know what they're working with.
  • Correct measurements: Always include your stats with your submission: height, measurements (bust/waist/hips for female models, chest/waist for male), shoe size, and eye colour. Dubai agencies are precise about this for commercial and fashion bookings.
  • Professionalism: The quality of the images signals your seriousness. Low-quality, poorly-lit portfolio images tell an agency something about how you approach your career.

The comp card: do you need one?

Yes. A comp card (also called a zed card or composite card) is a single-page document that shows 4 to 6 of your best images alongside your stats and contact information. It's the modelling equivalent of a business card: compact, professional, and what agencies send to clients when pitching you for a booking.

Standard comp card size is 5.5" x 8.5". The front usually has one strong hero image with your name. The back shows 3 to 5 images with your stats. Digital comp cards (PDF format) are increasingly common for email submissions, but printed versions still matter for in-person agency meetings.

Most photographers in Dubai who specialise in model portfolios can either design your comp card or refer you to someone who does.

After the shoot: submitting to agencies

Once you have your portfolio, approach agencies professionally. Dubai's main modelling agencies include MMG Model Management, Bareface Models, Deja Vu Agency, Lime Models, and FP Models, among others. Most accept submissions via their website or email.

Your submission should include:

  • A short, professional introduction (2 to 3 sentences: no life story)
  • Your stats (height, measurements, age, nationality)
  • A link to your online portfolio or attached comp card PDF
  • 5 to 8 of your strongest images attached directly to the email
  • Your availability and whether you're currently signed elsewhere

Follow up once after two weeks if you haven't heard back. Beyond that, move on and continue building your experience.

Building a modeling portfolio in Dubai is a real investment: of time, money, and intention. The market here is competitive, but it's also active, international, and genuinely diverse in the kind of work available. A strong portfolio, shot properly, opens doors that are otherwise closed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many photos should be in a modeling portfolio in Dubai?
A solid starting portfolio needs 10 to 15 strong images. Quality matters far more than quantity. Agencies typically want to see range: clean headshots, a full-length look, and 2 to 3 different moods or styles.
How much does a modeling portfolio photoshoot cost in Dubai?
Portfolio sessions in Dubai typically range from AED 800 to AED 3,500 depending on the photographer, number of looks, location, and whether hair and makeup are included. Avoid the cheapest options: your portfolio is a professional tool, not a hobby project.
Do I need a modeling agency before building a portfolio in Dubai?
No. Most agencies in Dubai require you to submit a portfolio before they'll sign you. Build the portfolio first, then approach agencies with something to show.
What should I wear for a modeling portfolio photoshoot in Dubai?
Bring at least 3 outfits: something fitted and neutral for a clean full-body look, one fashion-forward piece that shows your style, and casual everyday wear. Avoid heavy patterns or logos that pull attention away from you.
Can I build a modeling portfolio in Dubai without any experience?
Yes: and most people do. A good photographer will direct you through the entire session. You don't need to know how to pose before you walk in. That's the photographer's job.
