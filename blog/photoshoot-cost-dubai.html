Pricing Guide

How Much Does a Photoshoot
Cost in Dubai?

Written by Hatim
Published July 2026
Read time 9 min

"How much do you charge?" is the first question almost everyone asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on more than most people expect.

I get this question daily, from first-time models building a portfolio, from brands planning a campaign, and from people who just want a decent portrait for their LinkedIn or Instagram. And every time, the real answer is the same: it depends on what you actually need, not just how long you stand in front of a camera.

This guide breaks down real photography pricing in Dubai for 2026: what drives the number up, what brings it down, and what you should actually be comparing when you get quotes from different photographers. No vague ranges pulled from nowhere: just how pricing actually works in this market.

Why there's no single answer

Dubai's photography market is unusually wide. On one end, you have photographers just starting out, building a portfolio and pricing accordingly. On the other, you have photographers with a decade of published work, agency relationships, and a waitlist. Both call themselves photographers. Both will quote you a number. Those numbers can differ by a factor of five or more, and both can be entirely fair for what they include.

The type of shoot matters just as much as the photographer. A quick personal portrait session is a completely different product from a full-day brand campaign with a crew, multiple looks, and usage rights attached. Comparing the two on price alone is like comparing a taxi ride to a car lease: technically both get you somewhere, but they're not the same thing.

Worth knowing

In Dubai, a lot of photographers price by session length rather than a flat "per photo" rate. A common structure is a two hour block that includes a set number of professionally edited images: simple to understand, easy to compare, and it scales naturally with what you actually need.

What actually drives the price

Once you understand the variables, quotes stop feeling random. Here's what genuinely moves the number:

  1. Session length A one hour headshot session and a full-day editorial shoot are not priced on the same scale, and shouldn't be. Most portrait and portfolio sessions run one to two hours; brand and commercial work often runs longer.
  2. Number of edited images Raw files aren't the deliverable, edited ones are. The more final, retouched images you need, the more editing time is baked into the price. A handful of hero shots costs less than a full gallery of thirty.
  3. Location and setup A single outdoor location is simpler than a studio rental, multiple locations, or anything requiring lighting equipment and setup time. Travel between locations adds time that has to be accounted for.
  4. Hair, makeup, and styling If a session includes a makeup artist or stylist, that's a separate cost layered on top of photography, whether the photographer coordinates it or you bring your own.
  5. Usage rights This is the one people miss most. A personal portrait for your own social media is priced very differently from images a brand plans to use in paid advertising, on billboards, or across a national campaign. Commercial usage costs more because the client is paying for the right to use the images, not just the images themselves.
  6. Experience and demand A photographer who is booked out months in advance, with a strong portfolio and recognisable published work, will price accordingly. That's not just ego pricing: it reflects consistent, proven results and reduced risk for the client.

Rough market ranges

Treat these as a general orientation, not a quote. Every photographer prices differently, and the ranges below reflect the breadth of Dubai's market rather than any single photographer's rates.

Shoot type Typical duration What shifts it
Personal portrait / headshot 1–2 hours Number of edits, one location vs multiple
Model portfolio / test shoot 1.5–2.5 hours Outfit changes, makeup, whether it's TFP or paid
Couple / family session 1–2 hours Group size, location, number of final images
Brand / product content Half day to full day Usage rights, deliverable count, crew size
Event coverage 2+ hours Event length, number of photographers, turnaround time

For context: many established Dubai photographers structure their portrait and portfolio sessions around a two hour block, priced somewhere in the low hundreds of dirhams for that window. It's a useful benchmark to have in mind when you're comparing quotes, even though the exact number always shifts with the specifics above.

The cheapest quote and the most expensive quote can both be "reasonable." The question isn't which number is right: it's which one matches what you actually need.

What a two hour session usually gets you

Two hours is a common sweet spot in Dubai for portraits, portfolio shoots, and lifestyle content, and for good reason. It's enough time to plan two or three distinct looks or settings, work through nerves and warm up in front of the camera, and still leave with a genuinely usable set of final images: without the session dragging into diminishing returns.

What that usually includes:

  • A planned shot list covering two to three outfit or setting changes
  • Enough time to get comfortable in front of the camera, especially for first-timers
  • A set number of professionally edited, delivery-ready images
  • One location, or two if they're close together

What it usually doesn't include, unless specified upfront: hair and makeup, studio rental, multiple far-apart locations, or commercial usage rights. Those are add-ons, and a transparent photographer will list them separately rather than folding them silently into the base price.

Questions to ask before you book

Price alone tells you almost nothing until you know what's included. Before comparing quotes, ask each photographer:

  • How many final edited images are included, and how many raw or unedited files (if any)?
  • How long is the actual shoot, and how long is turnaround for edited images?
  • Is the location included, or is studio/venue rental extra?
  • Are hair and makeup included, or is that arranged separately?
  • What usage rights come with the images: personal use only, or commercial?
  • What happens if I need extra edited images afterward?

Once you have answers to those, comparing two quotes becomes a lot more honest. A lower number that excludes half of what you need isn't actually cheaper.

Red flags in pricing

Worth watching for

Be cautious of quotes with no mention of how many images are included, vague promises of "unlimited" edits with no turnaround timeline, or prices that seem dramatically below everyone else with no clear reason why. Photography pricing in Dubai varies, but it doesn't vary infinitely, and a number far outside the norm is usually explained by something missing from the deal.

Questions I get asked all the time

How much does a photoshoot cost in Dubai?
It depends heavily on the type of shoot, but a straightforward portrait session with an experienced photographer typically falls somewhere in the low hundreds of dirhams per hour, with most sessions booked for one to three hours. Brand and commercial shoots run higher once usage rights and production are factored in.
What affects the price of a photoshoot in Dubai?
The main factors are shoot length, number of final edited images, location, whether hair and makeup is included, usage rights for commercial work, and the photographer's experience level and demand.
Is a two hour session enough for a portfolio shoot?
Yes, for most portrait or portfolio purposes. Two hours is generally enough time for two to three outfit or setting changes and to walk away with a solid, usable set of final images, provided the shot list is planned in advance.
Do photographers in Dubai charge per hour or per package?
Both models exist. Many portrait and lifestyle photographers price by session length, commonly two hour blocks, which include a set number of edited images. Commercial work is usually quoted as a package reflecting usage and deliverables rather than time alone.
Why do some Dubai photographers charge so much more than others?
Experience, portfolio quality, editing style, equipment, and demand all play a role. A photographer who is fully booked months in advance with a strong published body of work will typically price higher than someone just starting out.
