Professional Headshots in Dubai:
The Complete Guide

Written by Hatim
Published August 2026
Read time 8 min

Your headshot is often the first impression a client, recruiter or collaborator has of you, long before they read a single word about your work.

In a city like Dubai, where LinkedIn, WhatsApp Business and company websites carry real weight, a dated or badly lit photo can quietly work against you. This guide covers everything you need to know before booking a professional headshot session in Dubai: what to wear, how the session runs, what it costs, and what actually separates a headshot that builds trust from one that does not.

1. Why your headshot matters more than you think

A headshot is usually the first piece of visual information someone has about you, and it is processed almost instantly. Before anyone reads your title, your bio or your experience, they have already formed an impression from your photo.

That single image now shows up in more places than most people account for:

  • LinkedIn and other professional networks
  • Company "About" or "Team" pages
  • Press features, interviews and speaker bios
  • Visa, government and professional licensing profiles
  • Conference badges and event materials

A phone selfie or an old, cropped group photo can work in a pinch, but it rarely matches the level of polish everything else about a professional profile is trying to project.

2. What to wear for a corporate headshot

Wardrobe rules for a headshot are stricter than for a general portrait, because the goal is credibility, not personality. A few guidelines hold up consistently:

  • Solid colours photograph more predictably than patterns, especially on video calls and small profile thumbnails
  • Structured shoulders (a blazer, a tailored jacket) read as more polished than soft, unstructured fabric
  • Darker tones such as black, navy and charcoal tend to suit a plain studio backdrop, while lighter neutrals photograph well against a brighter or outdoor background
  • Keep jewellery minimal, avoid anything that catches direct light and creates glare
  • Simple, well-groomed hair holds up better across multiple angles than an intricate style shot once and never repeated

Avoid clothing with visible logos or busy branding unless the headshot is specifically for that brand. It dates quickly and pulls attention away from your face, which is the actual subject of the photo.

Structured shoulders and a solid dark colour against a plain backdrop - a reliable, repeatable headshot look.

3. Studio versus on-location headshots

There are two broad directions a headshot can take, and choosing between them mostly comes down to where the image will be used.

Studio or plain backdrop

A clean, neutral background keeps every ounce of attention on your face and expression. It is the safest, most classic choice for LinkedIn, press bios and any use where consistency matters, and it holds up well if the photo needs to be reused across many different platforms and formats.

On-location, personal brand style

Shooting somewhere with real context, an office interior, a recognisable Dubai backdrop, a considered setting, adds a layer of personality and story. This suits founders, consultants and anyone building a personal brand where a bit more warmth and individuality is an asset rather than a distraction.

Neither is objectively better. A studio headshot is the safer, more universal option; an on-location shoot says more about who you are, at the cost of being slightly less neutral.

A bolder colour choice for a personal-brand look, still controlled against a plain studio background.

4. What happens during the session

A typical headshot session runs 30 to 45 minutes, which is enough time to work through direction, adjust lighting between looks, and capture a genuine range of expressions rather than one stiff, single frame.

  1. Brief and direction. A short conversation about how the images will be used shapes the crop, expression and mood for the session.
  2. First look. The primary outfit, usually the safest and most universally usable option, shot first while energy is highest.
  3. Outfit change, if planned. A second look for a different platform or purpose, if agreed in advance.
  4. Review and select. A quick check of the images on camera partway through, to confirm the direction is working before continuing.

Most clients leave with several dozen usable frames per look, from which a small final set is selected and retouched.

A second look mid-session - the outfit and energy shift while the lighting setup stays consistent.

5. Individual versus team headshot days

Companies planning headshots for a full team benefit from a slightly different approach than a single individual session. A team headshot day uses one consistent backdrop, one lighting setup and a fixed camera position, so every employee's photo matches across the company website, email signatures and LinkedIn profiles.

This is worth planning as its own booking rather than a series of separate individual sessions. Consistency across the set matters more than any single image standing out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a professional headshot session take?
Most headshot sessions run 30 to 45 minutes. That is enough time for one to two outfit changes, a short break to check the images on the back of the camera, and a few different poses and angles per look.
Can I get multiple outfit changes in one session?
Yes. Two looks is standard: one classic option for LinkedIn or a company profile, and one slightly different tone for a press bio, website or personal use. Bring outfits already tried on and tested rather than deciding on the day.
Do you offer packages for teams or companies?
Yes. Team headshot days use a single consistent backdrop and lighting setup so every employee's photo matches across the company website, email signatures and LinkedIn. Contact Hatim directly to plan a team session.
What is the difference between a headshot and a portrait?
A headshot is built to communicate credibility quickly, usually a tighter crop, plain or simple background, and direct eye contact. A portrait has more room for mood, environment and personal style. Many sessions capture both in the same booking.
How soon will I receive my final images?
Typical turnaround is 48 to 72 hours after the session, with a small set of fully retouched, ready-to-use images delivered digitally.
