On Shooting

Golden Hour in Dubai -
What Nobody Tells You

Written by Hatim
Published April 2025
Read time 6 min

Every photography account you follow makes golden hour in Dubai look effortless. It isn't. Here's what the edited reel leaves out.

The light in Dubai during golden hour is genuinely extraordinary. I'm not going to pretend otherwise - when it works, it works in a way that I haven't experienced anywhere else. The combination of dust particles in the air, the architecture catching warm directional light, the reflections off the water in the marina - you can produce images here that would be technically impossible to replicate in most other cities.

But "golden hour" in Dubai is also roughly 22 minutes long, it moves, and three times out of five something gets in the way of it.

The timing problem

Sunset in Dubai in summer happens around 7:15 PM. The light you actually want - that warm, directional, shadows-stretching-across-the-ground light - starts maybe twenty minutes before that and is largely gone eight minutes after. I'm not exaggerating that window. I've tracked this across dozens of shoots.

In winter it's more forgiving. November through February gives you a longer transition, the sun sits lower in the sky earlier, and the haze is often less severe. If you're planning a big portrait session specifically around golden hour and you have flexibility on timing, January or February is when I'd schedule it.

In summer? I'm usually not chasing golden hour at all. The heat at 6:30 PM in August is still around 40 degrees, which is genuinely miserable for subjects, makes mirrorless bodies unhappy after extended use, and means everyone in your frame - including your model - looks like they'd rather be somewhere else.

Where to actually shoot

I'll be honest about something: a lot of the Dubai golden hour content you see online is shot from the same four or five locations. The Burj Khalifa view from Downtown, the marina waterfront, the palm with the Atlantis framing, Jumeirah beach with the Burj Al Arab silhouette. These all produce beautiful images. They're also heavily trafficked, sometimes require permits that are difficult to obtain on short notice, and have enough foot traffic that you're constantly managing background pedestrians.

My preferred spots are less obvious. Al Quoz has industrial architecture that catches the light in interesting ways and nobody is competing with you for the position. The older parts of Deira - particularly near the creek - give you a completely different visual language, all texture and peeling paint and dhows on the water. For portraits where I want space and flexibility, some of the developing areas on the outskirts give you clean lines and minimal interference.

What I bring for golden hour sessions Main body with a 85mm f/1.4 - the compression and the way it handles the backlight is worth the weight. X100VI as second body for environmental shots. Collapsible reflector - one person can hold it but two is easier. Extra batteries, always. The heat drains them faster than you'd expect.

Permits are a real consideration. Dubai has specific regulations about commercial photography in public spaces, around certain landmarks, and in some cultural areas. For editorial work I do my research before the shoot and carry documentation. For personal or artistic shoots the situation is more relaxed, but I still avoid anything that could create a confrontational situation - that's not the energy you want going into a session.

Working with the light itself

Something that took me a while to learn: don't fight the direction. Dubai golden hour often puts you in a position where the ideal background means shooting directly into the light or very nearly so. A lot of photographers back away from this because backlit subjects are harder to expose correctly and autofocus can hunt.

I've come to love that situation. A subject backlit by a low Dubai sun, with the gold catching the edge of their hair and the city going warm and slightly hazy behind them - that's a frame you can't fake in a studio. The trick is having a reflector or a small portable light to fill the shadows on the face, and knowing your camera well enough to trust the metering or override it quickly.

Expose for the face, let the background blow slightly warm. You'll lose some detail in the highlights but you gain something that looks like the city actually is - luminous, a bit overwhelming, genuinely beautiful in the way that makes people move here and never quite leave.

Golden hour in Dubai is worth chasing. Just go in knowing that the window is short, the conditions are variable, and the images that look casual and easy usually had a lot of planning behind them. Arrive early to your location, know exactly where you're setting up before the light changes, and have your settings ready before you need them.

The twenty good minutes go fast.
