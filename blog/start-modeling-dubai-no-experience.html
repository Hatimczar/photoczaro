Modeling Guide

How to Start Modeling in Dubai
With No Experience

Written by Hatim
Published July 2026
Read time 10 min

Everyone in Dubai who works as a model today started exactly where you are now: with no credits, no agent, and no idea what the first step was supposed to be.

I've photographed models at every stage. First-timers who walked into a session nervous, unsure how to stand, holding their phone for reference poses they'd saved the night before. Six months later, some of those same people are signed, working regularly, and building careers they didn't think were possible. Others realised the reality didn't match the idea: and that's fine too.

What I've learned from all of it is that the path from zero to working model in Dubai is actually pretty clear. The problem isn't that it's complicated. The problem is that most of the information out there is either vague or designed to sell you something. This guide isn't either of those things.

Is Dubai a good place to start?

Yes: with caveats. Dubai is one of the most active commercial markets in the Middle East. Brands here need models constantly: product campaigns, hotel and hospitality work, real estate, fashion retail, fitness, events. There is genuine demand, year-round.

The caveats: it is also competitive, and the market is more commercial than creative. If you're imagining runway in Paris or editorials in New York, Dubai is a different beast. It rewards clean, versatile looks and professional behaviour over avant-garde edge. That's not a criticism: it's just useful to know going in.

Worth knowing

Dubai has a particularly strong market for commercial and lifestyle models: think brand campaigns, hotel photography, fitness content, and real estate marketing. If you fit that brief, the work here is consistent and well-paid compared to most markets.

Step one: Get honest about your starting point

Before you approach anyone: agency, photographer, or brand: be clear-eyed about what you're bringing to the table right now. Not in a self-critical way. In a practical way.

Ask yourself:

  • What type of modelling do I actually want to do? (Fashion, commercial, fitness, luxury, runway, social media)
  • Am I willing to invest time before I earn anything?
  • Do I have the right to work in the UAE, or am I on a tourist visa?
  • Am I prepared to hear constructive feedback and act on it?

None of these questions are dealbreakers. They just determine what your next 90 days look like. Someone on a tourist visa can still build a portfolio through test shoots: they just can't take paid work yet. Someone who wants runway needs different photos than someone targeting commercial brands.

Step two: Get some photos: the right way

This is where most beginners go wrong. They either skip photos entirely and approach agencies with selfies, or they spend a lot of money on the wrong kind of shoot before they know what they need.

Professional photography is not universally required for a first application. Simple, current phone photographs can be valid digitals if they meet the target agency's rules: clean light, a plain background, natural expression and no filters. Before you shoot anything, check the specific agency's own current instructions, since the number, angle and format they ask for can differ. As a general starting point, without a specific agency brief to follow, three types of image cover most of what's useful:

  1. A clean headshot Face clearly lit, minimal distractions, natural expression. This is what every agency looks at first. It should look like you: not a heavily filtered, overlit version of you.
  2. A full-length shot In fitted clothing, ideally something simple and neutral. The goal is to show your proportions clearly. This doesn't need to be fashion editorial: clean and honest is the right approach at this stage.
  3. One lifestyle or personality shot Something that shows energy or range: a smile, movement, or a different mood from the headshot. Agencies want to see that there's something there beyond just a face.

That covers most first submissions, but treat it as a starting point rather than a fixed rule. You don't need hair and makeup for this stage. You need clean, honest images that show what you actually look like, in whatever number and format the agency you're applying to actually asks for. For a fuller breakdown of digitals versus a styled portfolio and when you need each, see this guide to model digitals vs a model portfolio in Dubai.

The agencies that matter in Dubai are not looking for perfection in a first submission. They're looking for potential: and the ability to photograph well. Those are two different things.

Test shoots: how to build a portfolio for free

A test shoot: sometimes called TFP, for Time For Prints: is a mutually agreed collaboration where a photographer and model work together, with the scope and image usage confirmed in writing beforehand. In its classic form, no money changes hands: the photographer gets new portfolio content and the model gets usable images. That's not the only legitimate way to build a portfolio, though, and it shouldn't be assumed as a default. A paid portfolio session, where you book and pay a photographer directly, is a separate service and is often the more reliable route to images built specifically around your goals.

This is how almost every model in Dubai builds their first real portfolio. And it's entirely legitimate. Good photographers do test shoots throughout their careers to try new ideas and explore different looks. A beginner model with strong natural potential is exactly the kind of person many photographers are looking for.

How to find a test shoot

Start on Instagram. Look for photographers in Dubai whose work you genuinely like: whose style matches the kind of modelling you want to do. When you find someone, look at their recent work and check whether they do test shoots (many mention it in their bio or highlight stories).

When you reach out, keep it short and specific. Don't say "I want to model." Say: "I'm building my portfolio and love your work: particularly [specific series or style]. I'd love to discuss a test shoot if you're open to it." Include two or three of your best existing photos. Respect their time. Follow up once if you don't hear back, then move on.

Red flags to watch for

A single detail rarely proves someone is illegitimate on its own, including an upfront charge, since some legitimate paid sessions exist alongside free TFP collaborations. Watch instead for combinations of warning signs: an identity or business you can't verify, pressure to decide quickly, guaranteed work or agency signing, requests for nude or lingerie images at a scouting or "test" stage, no written agreement on scope and usage, unusual payment demands, or refusal to let you verify them through official agency or industry channels. Always bring a friend for early shoots if something feels off.

Approaching agencies in Dubai

Dubai has a solid cluster of agencies that actively sign new talent. The better-known names include ADAM Models, Anagram, MMG, and Star Model Management: but the landscape shifts, so do your own research before submitting. Look at who they currently represent and whether your look fits their board.

Most agencies accept submissions through their website or Instagram. The process is simple: send your photos, your measurements (height, bust/chest, waist, hips, shoe size), your age, and a brief note about your background. Don't overthink it. Don't write three paragraphs about your passion for modelling. Give them the information they need to make a quick decision.

Expect either silence, a rejection, or an invitation for a casting. A rejection isn't a verdict on your future: agencies are looking for specific gaps in their current roster. One agency passing means nothing about the next. Submit to five or six and see what comes back.

What they actually look at

  • Your photos: how you photograph, not just how you look
  • Your measurements: for fashion work especially, these determine what bookings you're right for
  • Your attitude in person: punctuality, professionalism, how you take direction at a casting
  • Your versatility: can you do more than one look or mood?

What they're not looking at, at least at first: your social media following, your experience level, or whether you've been published anywhere. Those things matter later. Right now, the photos and the in-person casting are everything.

What no one tells you about starting out

The beginning is slow. Almost always. There is a period: typically three to six months: where you're doing test shoots, approaching agencies, going to castings, and not booking much paid work. This is normal. It's not a sign you're on the wrong path. It's the path.

During that period, the most useful thing you can do is stay active. Keep doing test shoots to improve your portfolio. Go to every casting you're invited to, even ones that don't seem promising. Build relationships with photographers and creatives in the city. Dubai is a relatively small industry once you're in it: people remember reliability and professionalism more than almost anything else.

The models who build careers here aren't always the most naturally gifted. They're the ones who show up on time, take direction well, and keep going when it's quiet.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring warning signs around an agency: legitimate agencies typically make their money from commissions on your bookings. An upfront charge on its own isn't automatic proof of a scam, but watch for it alongside other signs: an identity you can't verify, guaranteed work, pressure to decide fast, requests for nude or lingerie scouting images, no written contract, or refusal to let you verify them through official channels.
  • Waiting until you have "better" photos: start now with what you have. Your photos will improve as you work.
  • Submitting the same photos to every agency: tailor your submission to each agency's aesthetic and client base.
  • Ghosting photographers or agencies: Dubai's industry is smaller than it looks. Unprofessionalism gets remembered.
  • Ignoring feedback: when a photographer or agent gives you specific feedback, that's valuable. Use it.
  • Working without a contract: for any paid work, get the terms in writing. What's being used, where, for how long, and for how much.

A realistic timeline

Here's what a realistic first year looks like for someone starting from zero in Dubai with genuine commitment:

  1. Month 1–2: Foundation Get your first set of professional photos through a test shoot. Research and submit to 5–6 agencies. Start building relationships with local photographers and creatives.
  2. Month 3–4: First castings If an agency signs you, you'll start attending castings. If not, keep submitting and keep doing test shoots to grow your portfolio. Social content, if that's your direction, can start here.
  3. Month 5–6: First bookings Paid work often starts to come in around this point: small commercial jobs, product shoots, lifestyle content. Rates at this stage are modest but the experience is building.
  4. Month 7–12: Momentum With consistent professionalism and a growing portfolio, repeat bookings start. Brands and photographers begin to request you specifically rather than through open castings. This is where the shift happens.

Some people move through this faster. Some slower. Both are fine. The timeline above isn't a deadline: it's a map.

Questions I get asked all the time

Do I need experience to start modeling in Dubai?
No. Most models in Dubai started with zero experience. What you need is a small set of current, clean photos that meet your target agency's requirements (phone photos can be enough at this stage), a willingness to do test shoots or a portfolio session, and realistic expectations about how quickly work comes in.
How do I approach a modeling agency in Dubai?
Most agencies accept online submissions through their website or Instagram. As a starting point, a clean headshot, a full-length shot, and one lifestyle image cover most of what's useful, but check the specific agency's current page for their exact photo count and format before you submit. Include your measurements and age, and keep the message short and professional.
What is a test shoot and do I need one?
A test shoot (TFP) is a mutually agreed collaboration, usually unpaid, where the scope and image usage are confirmed in writing and both sides get usable images. It's a common way models in Dubai build their first book, though a paid portfolio session is a legitimate alternative. Some form of portfolio material helps, whichever route you choose.
How old do you have to be to model in Dubai?
Most commercial and fashion agencies in Dubai sign models from 18 years old. Some accept 16 to 17 with parental consent. There is also a growing market for mature models (35+) in commercial and lifestyle work.
Do I need a visa to model in Dubai?
To work commercially in Dubai you need a valid UAE work visa or freelance permit. Many agencies help with this once they sign you. For test shoots and building a portfolio, a tourist visa is fine: you just can't accept paid bookings on it.
